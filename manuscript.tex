\section{Theory}
\subsection{eyes tracking}
\subsubsection{background}
To find the begining of this topic, I always wanna how to describe what we can see in real world
and also what kinds of factors would influence them, so I find that Watson and Yelot (2012) conducted a systematic and comprehensive study to explore the 
effects of visual conditions and other factors (monocular vision and binocular vision, 
brightness, field size, age, adaptation status, cognitive load, etc.) 
on the pupil's light response. To be more specific, this is waht they explore
\begin{equation}
    D_{sd}(F) = 7.75 - 5.75 \times \frac{(F/846)^{0.41}}{(F/846)^{0.41} + 2}
    \label{eq:watson_yellott}
\end{equation}

where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $D_{sd}(F)$ represents the purely physiological target pupil diameter, the size of which is driven entirely by the physical properties of ambient light.
    \item $F$ denotes the effective retinal illuminance, measured in Trolands (Td).
\end{itemize}

The Watson-Yellott pupil dynamics formula (Equation \ref{eq:watson_yellott}) functions as the system's internal ``light meter.'' Within the \texttt{lens\_deform.comp} module, this equation is utilized to convert the intensity of the external stimulus light source into effective retinal illuminance, thereby calculating the target physiological pupil diameter strictly as a function of environmental lighting conditions.
Based on that, to the next step, \cite{seyedmadani2025effects} demonstrate that 
binocularity and eye-dominance-modulated retinal spatial non-uniformities jointly 
shape the natural nonlinear variations in visual-motor tracking, providing a 
crucial baseline for understanding visual neural mechanisms and clinically 
detecting neuro-ophthalmological impairments.
\subsubsection{simulation}
Next step, I also find some equation or method to construct pupil expense and reflection in lens
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Asymmetric Damped Spring Filter:} 
    $$ \alpha = 1 - \exp\left(-\frac{dt}{\tau}\right) $$
    It simulates muscle dynamics, giving the pupil's light response realistic latency and biological inertia.
    
    \item \textbf{Paraxial Mirror Equation:} 
    $$ v = \frac{uR}{2u - R} $$
    It bypasses computationally expensive ray tracing to directly calculate the precise 3D coordinates of the Purkinje reflections.
    
    \item \textbf{Schlick's Fresnel Approximation:} 
    $$ F(\theta) = R_0 + (1 - R_0)(1 - \cos\theta)^5 $$
    It rapidly computes surface reflectance based on viewing angles, perfectly recreating the bright corneal and dim lenticular optical layers.
\end{itemize}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{plan}

The precise prediction of accommodation and vergence heavily depends on the quality of the synthesized ocular reflections. 
Historically, researchers have employed Zemax for such optical simulations; however, we found that traditional ray-tracing software 
is computationally exhaustive and yields suboptimal visual fidelity for dynamic Purkinje image generation.

To optimize both rendering time and optical effect, we constructed our simulation pipeline using Blender's advanced rendering engine.
By transitioning from conventional PCCR—which infers accommodation from fixation bias—to a DPI-centric approach within this modern 
rendering framework, we achieved superior optical sampling rates. The updated software allows for more efficient multi-path light 
tracing, enabling the extraction of high-resolution Purkinje images that accurately represent the eye's physical accommodation state.

\subsection{In-vivo Data Acquisition and Multimodal Inference}
Building upon our established hardware framework, we extend our methodology to capture empirical in-vivo data from human subjects.
To bridge this physical hardware with state-of-the-art computational paradigms, we conceptualize an intelligent diagnostic agent 
capable of automated clinical evaluation and the generation of plausible synthetic datasets for both medical and consumer 
applications. As a proof-of-concept for this integration, we formulate a novel inferential pipeline: raw optical captures 
acquired directly from the hardware are fed into a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM). Rather than relying solely on 
traditional analytical algorithms, we leverage the MLLM's emergent reasoning capabilities to infer critical ocular properties,
estimating physiological metrics such as vergence and dynamic accommodation states directly from the visual inputs.
